Digital creature living in your browser

Crawlers is a kinetic, interactive work with feedback loops operating on multiple levels. Its motion system combines algorithmic and nature-inspired distributed models, inviting viewers to explore the underlying mechanisms. Its visual language reflects a similar balance: points, squares, and circles combine rigid technical expression with dynamic, lifelike movement.

The creature is rendered as a small three-dimensional organism above an ordinary two-dimensional document. The page remains readable and scrollable beneath it. Its body has volume, a visible carapace, a rounded abdomen, articulated joints, surface hairs, and a warm pattern of amber, caramel, cream, and espresso brown. The ten-limbed form is an artistic extension of a spider-inspired crawler, not a claim that the biological Black House Spider has ten walking legs.

Interaction with the page attempts to blur the boundaries between the artwork and the viewer, while also reflecting on contemporary questions surrounding web crawlers, bots, and data collection. Page generation introduces a simple generative system, a stripped-down language-model predictor, offering viewers an opportunity to experience machine learning at its core.

What happens when a digital object behaves as though the page beneath it were a physical world?

Movement as a distributed system

The crawler does not travel along a single rigid animation path. Its limbs move in offset phases. Some feet remain planted while others lift, reach, and touch down. The body follows the changing direction of travel with a small delay, creating the impression that the legs coordinate locally rather than obeying one perfectly synchronized command.

When a foot lands, the crawler briefly marks the word or page element beneath it. Text takes on a warm copper color; images, controls, icons and other elements receive a subtle contact outline. The page remains ordinary HTML, but the crawler treats its visible DOM as terrain. The cursor becomes another environmental signal. Move it across the page and the creature will investigate; leave it alone and it resumes exploring the nearby text.

Contact, friction, and attention

Each leg has a proximal attachment, a stout upper section, a compact knee-like transition, a longer middle section, and progressively finer distal segments. Its joints bend independently to suggest weight transfer. The planted feet are spread away from the body to create a broad support polygon, while the lifted feet arc forward before touching down again.

The body is free to turn, lean, climb sideways, and briefly invert. These gestures do not change the document itself; they change the creature's orientation relative to the page. In a larger installation, the same surface-following logic could be extended to cards, images, navigation regions, page edges, and other elements in a site's DOM.

On the boundary between organism and interface

In the age of AI agents, we increasingly find ourselves personifying probabilistic algorithms. The work invites us to reflect on this tendency and consider deeper questions about what is alive, what is not, and what we are. How are we different from machines?

A browser crawler is not alive. It has no metabolism, nervous system, or independent intention. Yet a moving body with pauses, corrections, exploratory gestures, and a response to touch can prompt us to attribute intention to it. The experience sits between a biological metaphor and a procedural system.

How to interact with your crawler

1. Wake it up

Move your pointer through the article. The crawler notices recent motion and approaches the pointer. It does not block clicks, text selection, or scrolling, so the document remains a normal webpage while the creature moves above it.

2. Let it explore

Stop moving the pointer. The creature will select nearby words and travel from one text region to another. Watch the feet: planted legs spread outward to support the body, while lifted legs reach forward and settle onto a new point.

3. Change the terrain

Scroll the page. The text moves in two dimensions while the crawler remains a three-dimensional object in the browser's viewing space. Its target positions are recalculated from the visible document, so it can continue walking over new paragraphs as they enter the viewport.

4. Watch it climb and hang

From time to time the crawler changes its body roll and leg posture. It leans as though crossing a vertical edge, turns onto its side, and briefly hangs inverted before returning to its ordinary walking orientation. The animation is a visual surface-walking study rather than a biomechanically validated simulation of every possible spider gait.

Field observation

The crawler is intentionally small compared with the page. The article remains the primary visual layer; the creature is a visitor, not a replacement for the content.

Look closely
Ten legs move in staggered phases. Watch for a planted stance, a lifted reach, and short copper flashes where feet contact words.

The page text is 2D HTML. The crawler is a 3D mesh rendered in a transparent canvas above it.
